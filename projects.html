Oregon high school tennis rankings

Win-loss records ignore who you played and count every flight the same, so a coach could stack a lineup to look better. My flight-weighted score weights the eight flights from 1.00 down to 0.10, so depth counts and stacking stops paying. The Power Index blends it with an RPI-style strength of schedule, and the OSAA now seeds its dual team championship from it.

PI = 0.65·APR + 0.25·FQI + 0.10·oGS FQI = (Σ flight_score·opp_strength + 5×0.5) / (duals + 5)

    Viperball kick decisions

    Viperball has six downs to gain 20 yards, and the ball moves by running, lateral chains, and kick passes. A live-play drop kick is worth 5 and a place kick 3. Every rule creates a strategy, so on a late down the coach weighs both kicks against going for it.

    distance = (100 − field position) + 10 drop kick EV = 5 × P(drop) place kick EV = 3 × P(place)

    Drag the marker to change the kick distance. Highlighted line: drop kick. Plain line: place kick.

    The call: GO FOR IT

    Early downs gain about 2 yards against 20 to go, so drives reach fifth and sixth down, where this call gets made. A missed drop kick on the first five downs usually keeps the ball.

    O27 college prospects

    In O27, a prospect’s box-score grade and actual ceiling are different things. Each player has a hidden ceiling and a fixed lens on it, so the grade you see is ceiling times lens. Two scouting reports estimate that ceiling with their own errors. I kept the lens fixed through college: a hidden gem can still look ordinary when draft day arrives. The decision is whether to trust the box score or two imperfect scouting reports. I explain the model in the development write-up.

    shown = round(ceiling × lens) lens 0.40–0.95 growth = min(rate × ceiling, cap) cap 15 → 4 scout = ceiling ± fog fog 7–31

    Pick the one you’d draft, then sign them and the lens drops. The plain bar is the box score. The highlighted bar is the real grade.

    Each player’s development rate is set when he’s generated. Nobody regresses. Everyone develops. The mystery is in who develops fast and how much they can hide it while getting there.
    Source: hybrid-baseball/o27v2/college.py and college_potential.py. These four prospects were generated by the game's own generate_college_player and advance_one_year (seeds 330, 245, 20, 219); reports drawn with make_scouting_report. Players get three growth years before they can sign.

    Play To Clinch point engine

    College tennis is a team sport: three doubles matches make one point, six singles matches are a point each, and the first team to four wins. My engine plays every point, so a small edge per point adds up over a match.

    P(server wins rally) = logistic(0.9·Δrally + 0.36 first | 0.10 second + …)

    Play To Clinch high school careers

    The first high school model put seniors in 85.7% of No. 1 singles spots. I compared that with six seasons of Oregon boys tennis, then rebuilt development around starting ability, career peak, annual growth, and actual playing time. JV matches count for half the exposure of varsity, so five freshmen with the same ceiling can end up with very different careers.

    gain = year's budget × playing time playing time: bench 0.55 · full JV 0.81 · varsity 1.00

    You Go First match score

    Scores are asymmetric. What you want, 10 chips across five traits, is scored against what they are, a behavioral quiz: 0.7 × alignment + 0.3 × temperature. Move the chips and both scores change.

    score = 0.7 × alignment + 0.3 × temperature temperature = max(0, 100 − 2 × |quiz gap|)
