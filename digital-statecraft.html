Digital Statecraft

The future of government is a design problem.

Technology is changing what institutions can do, what people expect of them, and who holds power when decisions move from public officials to software. Governments are investing in AI, replacing decades-old systems, and promising faster, more responsive services. The harder questions concern what they can deliver, how they exercise authority, and whether their institutions are prepared for what comes next.

My work in digital statecraft brings together product leadership, public administration, service design, and critical research. I’m interested in how institutions build the capacity to act, how they make consequential technology decisions, and what happens when ambitious plans meet the machinery of government.

That includes the practical work of de-risking technology investments, building effective teams, and improving public services. It also means questioning the promises that drive technological change.

Hype shapes budgets, policy agendas, and institutional priorities long before anyone knows whether a new technology will deliver what its advocates claim.

I bring these questions into talks, executive discussions, classrooms, and working sessions. Some examine how governments can become more inventive and capable. Others explore AI, public power, organizational failure, and the consequences of decisions made today.
